What we do

Research and advisory for decision-makers

Four service lines for NGOs, think tanks, diplomatic missions and multilateral partners. Each project is scoped around a specific question and a specific reader.

Research

Policy Research & Regional Briefings

  • Conflict-sensitivity and climate-security assessmentsField-informed analysis of climate, conflict and arms dynamics across East Africa, the Horn of Africa and the Sahel.
  • Emerging tech and arms control briefsPolicy papers and regulatory tracking on lethal autonomous weapons (LAWS), armed drones, military AI governance and illicit small arms proliferation (SALW).

Advisory

Advisory & Programme Design

  • WPS and YPS integrationAuditing and updating project designs, National Action Plan inputs and peacebuilding programmes so that women and youth are substantively included.
  • Security sector and peace operations advisoryCurriculum development, training-needs assessments, and policy guidance on atrocity prevention, civilian protection and climate-proofing peace operations.

Convening

Stakeholder Convening & Dialogue Facilitation

  • Geneva-to-Nairobi policy bridgingTranslating international negotiations (UNGA, CCW, ATT, COP) into regional dialogues, roundtables and civil society consultations in East Africa.
  • Multi-stakeholder workshopsExchanges between local peacebuilders, researchers, diplomatic missions and regional blocs (IGAD, AU, EAC).

Communications

Strategic Communications & Thought Leadership

  • Bilingual policy translation (English and French)Executive summaries, advocacy toolkits and high-level diplomatic correspondence connecting Anglophone and Francophone African security networks.

How we work

Standards we hold ourselves to

Sourced

Every figure carries a named source, and we say where a claim is reported rather than confirmed.

Regionally grounded

Analysis starts from East Africa, the Horn and the Sahel, then connects to Geneva and New York.

Bilingual

English and French, so that Anglophone and Francophone networks read the same analysis.

Written for a reader

Each product is built for one decision-maker and one decision, and kept short.

Founder

Agnes Nicole

Agnes founded Themis to bring a young African voice into arms control, climate security and technology governance. She is an Official Ambassador of the International Organization of Youth, a UNITAR fellow, and begins an MA in Diplomacy, Intelligence and Security at Strathmore University in January 2027.

Themis takes its name from the Greek personification of justice and order. That is the standard we work to: rules that are fair, enforced and made with those they affect.

Her recent writing covers multilateral deadlock at the CCW and COP31, and arms supply and Women, Peace and Security in Sudan. She has completed UNODA training on autonomous weapons and the Arms Trade Treaty, and earlier co-authored evidence briefs at the International Peace Support Training Centre.
