What we do

Research and advisory, built for decision-makers

Four service lines for NGOs, think tanks, diplomatic missions and multilateral partners. Each project is scoped around a specific question and a specific reader.

Research

Policy Research & Regional Briefings

Our thinking

The climate–conflict–arms feedback loop

Across the Horn of Africa and the Sahel, drought breaks customary systems of mediation. Armed groups and illicit weapons fill the gap, and violence then blocks the adaptation that could end the cycle. Breaking it means treating adaptation as conflict prevention and arms control as part of climate policy.

1 Ecological shockDrought collapses herds and harvests
2 RecruitmentStranded youth targeted by armed groups
4 Blocked adaptationViolence closes pastures and stalls projects
3 Arms as escalatorIllicit small arms turn disputes lethal

Founder

Agnes Nicole

Agnes founded Themis to bring a young African voice into arms control, climate security and technology governance. She is an Official Ambassador of the International Organization of Youth, a UNITAR fellow, and begins an MA in Diplomacy, Intelligence and Security at Strathmore University in January 2027.

Her recent writing covers multilateral deadlock at the CCW and COP31, and arms supply and Women, Peace and Security in Sudan. She has completed UNODA training on autonomous weapons and the Arms Trade Treaty.
